# Boiling water — vertical short (9:16), three storylines

Assumptions for all three: HyperFrames only (no Blender), 1080×1920, 30 fps, 35–45 s, burned-in captions,
voiceover (language to confirm), synthesized music bed + bundled sound effects, all free.

Facts used (checked):
- 250 mL of water ≈ 8.4 × 10²⁴ molecules (250 g ÷ 18.015 g/mol × 6.022 × 10²³)
- At 100 °C and 1 atm, steam takes ~1,600× the volume of the liquid (958.4 ÷ 0.598 kg/m³)
- Mean spacing between molecules: ~0.3 nm in the liquid, ~3.7 nm in the vapour
- Boiling 1 kg of water away (2,257 kJ) takes ~6.7× the energy of heating it from 20 to 100 °C (334 kJ)
- Boiling breaks hydrogen bonds *between* molecules; the O–H bonds *inside* each molecule stay intact
- The white cloud above a kettle is condensed droplets; water vapour itself is invisible

## A. The 100 °C mystery (recommended: a question everyone has seen, answered by the molecules)
Device: a question with the input. Form: an endless zoom.
- First 2 s: a pot on a flame, thermometer reading 100 °C. Text: "The fire's still on. Why won't it go past 100 °C?"
- Beats: hook → zoom into the water → one H₂O molecule (O + 2 H, shared electrons, δ− / δ+) →
  why neighbours stick (hydrogen bonds) → heat makes them shake → at 100 °C the energy goes into breaking
  hydrogen bonds, not raising temperature → molecules escape → payoff: 1 cup of water becomes ~1,600 cups of steam →
  claim: "100 °C is where heat stops warming water and starts freeing it."
- Look: deep water-blue ground, molecules as clean flat discs with soft shading, one hot accent (orange) for energy.
- Sound: low pulse that tightens as the thermometer climbs, a hit on the "stuck at 100" beat.

## B. That isn't steam
Device: life before and after (a myth turned over). Form: match cuts.
- First 2 s: a kettle with a white plume. The word "STEAM" types over it, then gets struck through.
- Beats: hook → the clear gap right at the spout is the real steam (invisible vapour) → zoom into the gap: molecules far apart →
  zoom into the white plume: molecules clumping back into droplets → back down into the boiling water to show where vapour comes from →
  claim: "Real steam is invisible. What you see is water coming back."
- Look: kitchen-white ground, strong black type, cyan for vapour, white for droplets.
- Sound: kettle whistle cut short by a record-scratch-free click on the strike-through.

## C. One molecule's escape
Device: a character does the work. Form: one continuous take.
- First 2 s: one H₂O molecule (named "H₂O #1") jammed in a crowd, held by three hydrogen bonds. Text: "Meet one water molecule. It's stuck."
- Beats: hook → its neighbours tug at it → heat arrives, everyone shakes → bonds snap one by one → it breaks free and flies up through
  a bubble → payoff: it bursts out of the surface into open air → claim: "Boiling is billions of molecules doing this every second."
- Look: the molecule is a character with a slight squash on each tug; warm accent only when it escapes.
- Sound: tension strings on each tug, a release on the escape.
